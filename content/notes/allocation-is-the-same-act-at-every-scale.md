A person decides where to put their time, attention and money. A family decides which child goes to which school and who takes over the farm. A tribe decides who hunts and who stays. A company decides which products to fund, a bank which companies, a society which industries. At every scale it is the same act: placing bets on the future with limited resources, and finding some way to share the losses of the bets that fail. It is one more pattern that holds at every scale, which is the reason I trust it, as I explain in [Everything is a fractal](/notes/everything-is-a-fractal/).

Joseph Schumpeter described the banker as the one who "authorises people, in the name of society as it were," to carry out new ventures, "the ephor of the exchange economy". Insurers do the other half, spreading the losses of the few across the many, which Taleb, in *Skin in the Game*, notes works informally inside families and in-groups but "can only work for a limited number of people" until someone universalises it. Banks and insurers are the institutional form of decisions every household also makes, and that is why I think they deserve to be understood as what they are, which is the argument of [The Original Data Company](/articles/the-original-data-company/).
