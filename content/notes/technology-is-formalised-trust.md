The definition of technology I keep coming back to has nothing to do with chips or apps. Technology is the formalisation of trust into procedure: taking something you would otherwise have to believe, a promise, a record, a judgement, and turning it into something that happens the same way every time and can be checked. A ledger is technology in that sense, double-entry bookkeeping is, a database transaction is, and so is a workflow engine or a type checker. What they share is that they replace "trust me" with "check it", which is the move I described in [Trust is earned by sameness](/notes/trust-is-earned-by-sameness/).

The definition has an uncomfortable consequence for anyone who says "we are not a tech company" while selling promises. If your product is a kept promise, then the machinery that keeps it, the models, procedures and records, is the product, and you have been a technology company all along. I made that argument for insurers and banks in [The Original Data Company](/articles/the-original-data-company/). It applies to anything abstract that people pay for because they trust it will behave the same way tomorrow.
