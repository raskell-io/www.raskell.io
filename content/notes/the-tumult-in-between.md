The two answers people reach for are both answers to the end state. Universal basic income is the money-focused one: if the economy produces enough, pay everyone a floor. Basic access to goods and services is the resource-focused one: if things become nearly free, guarantee the things rather than the money. Each has its advocates and each frightens a lot of people, and neither says anything about the interval.

The interval is the part I cannot see through. If AI really does lead to something like a post-scarcity economy, where most things are virtually free, the jobs go first and the abundance comes later, and in between there is a stretch where people lose their livelihoods at scale with nothing yet in place, and no shared view of where the collective ship is steering. That stretch will be chaotic, and I do not know how long it is. [The move to the city](/notes/the-move-to-the-city/) says such intervals end well; it does not say they are short. The most constructive response to it I have heard is [citizen entrepreneurs](/notes/citizen-entrepreneurs/).
