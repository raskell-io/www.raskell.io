Chapter 13 of Nassim Nicholas Taleb's *Skin in the Game*, "The Merchandising of Virtue", contains the line I wish I had remembered from my first reading: *"Courage is the only virtue you cannot fake."* Everything else can be performed. Generosity can be a press release, kindness can be a strategy, an opinion can be shared when it is safe to share. Courage costs something by definition, and the cost is what makes it real.

Taleb's test for virtue follows from that. *"Sticking up for truth when it is unpopular is far more of a virtue, because it costs you something,"* he writes, and the price is usually your reputation. Joining a crowd that is already shaming someone is the opposite: safe, popular, and in his words *"a mixture of bullying and cowardice."*

The part I keep coming back to is his advice to young people who come to him wanting to reduce poverty or save the world. He gives them three rules:

1. *"Never engage in virtue signaling"*
2. *"Never engage in rent-seeking"*
3. *"You must start a business. Put yourself on the line, start a business."*

Take bounded risks, he says, and if you get rich, which is optional, spend it generously on others. *"Courage (risk taking) is the highest virtue. We need entrepreneurs."*

I agree with this more than with most advice on how to do good. It matches what I believe about [who carries progress](@/notes/who-carries-progress.md): not the people with the best intentions about the world in general, but the people who put themselves into one concrete thing and answer for it. It is also what I mean when I say "skin in the game" in everyday speech, as I wrote in my [review of the book](@/articles/review-skin-in-the-game.md): ownership, daring, and enough of yourself in something that it can make the world a little better.
