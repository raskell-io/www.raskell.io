A friend of mine, an enterprise architect at a large Swiss energy group, answers [the tumult in between](/notes/the-tumult-in-between/) with the observation that the same tools that threaten jobs make citizen entrepreneurs possible. An ordinary person can use AI to identify a business opportunity, use AI to build the business, and use AI to operate it. The barrier was never only money; it was the ten different competences a business needs on day one, and those are now available by the hour. He took his own idea seriously enough to build a business idea factory, a system that generates and evaluates opportunities. About a week before we talked, someone else announced a project with the same shape on Hacker News, which he took as confirmation rather than competition.

I find it the most hopeful reading of [the window](/notes/the-window-before-the-utility/), and the one with the most work in it. It assumes the capability really is [democratised](/notes/a-capability-democratised/), which I believe, and it assumes people will take it up, which is the part the window is for.
