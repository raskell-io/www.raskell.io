Running through *The Infinity Machine* is a strand of belief I recognise: that intelligence built outside biology is coming, that it will carry us past limits we have treated as given, and that building it is among the most human things we could do. In the book the strand comes from science fiction, from the Culture novels Hassabis read as a teenager, and from the Singularity Summit with its supplements and its rapture, and Hassabis keeps a dry British distance from the Californian version while sharing its core. I share the core too, and I keep a different distance.

My spin comes from the other end of the timeline. I have spent years reading the ancient creation traditions through one working hypothesis, that the makers those traditions describe were a small, advanced civilisation that designed life here, and whatever one makes of that reading, it changes what transhumanism feels like from the inside. If it is right, then minds making minds is not a first. It is a recurrence, and we are the second iteration of a loop that someone else ran before us. The future stops being a rapture and becomes an inheritance, with the responsibilities inheritances carry: makers in a line of makers, for whom the question is not whether to build but how to be a good one, a question the old texts have a great deal to say about and the summit circuit nothing at all.

I am not going to argue the hypothesis in a note; it has a long home of its own elsewhere, and this site is not it. But the strand is there in everything I build, from a dish of simulated neurons to a chess engine that teaches itself, and it seemed dishonest to write about Hassabis's beliefs without owning mine. The long version is a piece for another year.
